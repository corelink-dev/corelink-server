## 1. Roles and responsibilities

1. **Controller / Processor / Sub-processor.** Controller acts as data controller
   (LGPD: *controlador*); Processor acts as data processor (LGPD: *operador*).
   Sub-processors listed in `legal/dpa/SUB-PROCESSOR-COMMITMENTS.md` act as
   sub-processors of the Processor.
2. **Documented instructions.** Processor processes personal data only on the
   Controller's documented instructions (Article 28(3)(a) GDPR; LGPD Art. 39).
   Acceptance of this DPA and use of the CoreLink service are the baseline
   documented instructions; further written instructions may be given via the
   master agreement, support tickets, or admin-plane configuration.
3. **Lawfulness.** Controller is responsible for the lawfulness of the
   personal data it instructs Processor to process and for obtaining all
   required data-subject notices and consents.
4. **Confidentiality.** Processor ensures personnel authorized to process
   personal data are bound by confidentiality (Art. 28(3)(b) GDPR).
5. **Compliance assistance.** Processor assists Controller in fulfilling its
   obligations under Articles 32–36 GDPR and LGPD Articles 46–49 (security,
   breach notification, DPIA cooperation, supervisory-authority cooperation).

---

## 2. Subject matter, duration, nature, and purpose

1. **Subject matter.** Processing of personal data through the CoreLink shared
   content-addressable cache service.
2. **Duration.** From the effective date of the master agreement until
   termination + the return/deletion period in §13.
3. **Nature of processing.** Storage, retrieval, deduplication, integrity
   verification (BLAKE3), audit logging, and DSR fulfillment.
4. **Purpose.** Deliver the CoreLink service to Controller. No secondary use,
   profiling, training of generic ML models, or onward disclosure for the
   benefit of any third party.

Processor will notify Controller **at least 30 calendar days** before adding,

### 3.2 Objection right

Controller may object on reasonable grounds within 30 days of the notice. If
the parties cannot agree on a resolution within an additional 30 days,
Controller may terminate the affected service component without penalty.

## 5. Retention

1. **Active service.** Personal data is retained while the service is active per
   Controller's configuration.
2. **Audit chain.** The launch service has no verified production path providing R2 Object Lock COMPLIANCE retention. Seven-year Object Lock retention and storage-enforced immutability are not available or promised. This draft does not establish an audit-event retention duration.
3. **Billing records.** Retained for **7 years** as required by US tax law and
   LGPD Art. 16 (legitimate interest, accounting).
4. **DSR erasure.** Personal data is erased per the DSR pipeline within the
   SLA freshness window (≤ 30 d Pro/Enterprise; ≤ 60 d Free/Starter; see SLA §2).
5. **Backups.** Backup retention is 90 days; erasure is propagated on the next
   backup cycle and tracked via the erasure attestation chain.

## 6. International transfers (Schrems II + LGPD Art. 33)

1. **Prelaunch shared-D1 disclosure.** Five production Worker environments
   reference one shared D1 control-plane database without a D1 jurisdiction
   binding. It contains tenant, membership, PAT, quota, billing-state, and
   audit-outbox records. A read-only Wrangler 4.145.0 readback on 2026-09-30 identified
   `corelink-prod-d1` (ID `d64742ea-e102-40b2-a844-ff02e3f94562`), with
   `running_in_region=ENAM`, `jurisdiction=null`, automatic read replication,
   and 162 tables. The reported region is metadata, not a physical-location
   guarantee. This is the technical launch posture selected by the root owner,
   not legal approval or an executed customer commitment.
2. **Transfer basis pending.** SCCs, the UK IDTA, and LGPD Art. 33 mechanisms
   are references for counsel assessment. No transfer basis or safeguard for
   this shared D1 posture has been approved or executed. This prelaunch copy
   does not incorporate an effective customer transfer instrument.
3. **TIA pending.** The assessment in `legal/tia-template.md` is incomplete.
   Counsel must assess the shared D1 control plane and approve applicable
   safeguards before any customer-facing transfer commitment is published.
4. **Government access requests.** Processor publishes annual transparency
   reporting. Processor will use all lawful means to challenge overbroad
   government access requests targeting Controller's personal data and notify
   Controller unless legally prohibited.
5. **Residency scope.** Tenant-pinned R2/DO source paths do not establish
   placement for the shared D1 control plane. This prelaunch copy makes no
   tenant-specific D1 location or no-transfer guarantee.

## 7. Data subject rights (DSR) cooperation

1. **Assistance scope.** Processor assists Controller in complying with
   data-subject rights of access, rectification, erasure, restriction,
   portability, and objection (GDPR Arts. 12–22; LGPD Art. 18; CCPA §1798.100
   et seq.).
2. **DSR pipeline.** Processor exposes the DSR pipeline (S-11) which Controller
   can integrate via API; Processor's response SLA is per SLA §2 (freshness).
3. **Cost.** Reasonable DSR cooperation is included in the service fee.
   Manifestly excessive requests may be subject to fees per the master agreement.
4. **Notice forwarding.** If Processor receives a data-subject request directly,
   Processor will refer the data subject to Controller and notify Controller
   within 5 business days unless legally prohibited.

---

## 8. Security measures

1. Processor implements appropriate technical and organizational measures per
   Art. 32 GDPR / LGPD Art. 46. The current measures are documented in
   `specs/03_architecture/security_model.md` and the controls
   catalogued in `specs/03_architecture/compliance_matrix.md`.
2. **Encryption.** In transit, TLS 1.2 is the minimum; TLS 1.3 is negotiated
   where supported. At rest, provider-managed R2 encryption applies. BYOK,
   customer-managed keys, and per-tenant CMK separation are unavailable at
   launch and are not represented as active controls.
3. **Pseudonymization.** Access tokens are Argon2id-hashed; audit-chain entries
   reference tenant-scoped identifiers.
4. **Ongoing CIA.** Continuous monitoring (Drata/Vanta); SOC 2 Type II target
   by S-21; ISO 27001 alignment.
5. **Resilience.** Multi-region active-active with PAT-REGION-FAILOVER-001.
6. **Testing.** Annual external penetration test; weekly chaos drills.

## 9. Personal data breach notification (72 hours)

1. **Internal escalation.** Processor's incident-response runbook escalates
   suspected personal-data breaches within **24 hours** of detection
   (`specs/_runbooks/RB-INCIDENT-RESPONSE.md`).
2. **Customer notification.** Processor notifies Controller without undue
   delay and in any case within **72 hours** of becoming aware of a
   personal-data breach (Art. 33 GDPR; LGPD Art. 48).
3. **Notification content.** Nature of breach; categories and approximate
   number of data subjects and records affected; likely consequences;
   measures taken or proposed. Where information cannot be provided at the
   same time, it will be provided in phases without further undue delay.
4. **Cooperation.** Processor assists Controller in supervisory-authority
   notification and data-subject notification where the breach is likely to
   result in a high risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons.

## 10. Audit rights

1. **Documentary audits.** Processor makes available SOC 2 Type II reports
   (when issued), ISO 27001 certification artifacts, the canonical compliance
   matrix (`specs/03_architecture/compliance_matrix.md`), and audit-log
   exports on reasonable notice.
2. **On-site audits.** Once per 12-month period, on **30 days** prior written
   notice, during business hours, at Controller's reasonable expense, and
   subject to confidentiality and reasonable security protocols. Regulators
   may audit as required by law without these limits.
3. **Frequency adjustment.** Where required by Controller's regulator or by a
   material change in security posture, audits may occur more frequently.
4. **Audit findings.** Processor will remediate confirmed material findings
   within a mutually agreed timeline.

## 12. Term and termination

1. **Term.** This DPA is effective on the master-agreement effective date and
   remains in force as long as Processor processes personal data on behalf of
   Controller.
2. **Termination.** Either party may terminate this DPA for material breach on
   30 days written notice and an opportunity to cure (where curable).
3. **Survival.** §5 (retention), §6 (international transfers — return/transit),
   §9 (breach notification — final), §13 (return/deletion), §15 (governing
   law), and any cure obligations survive termination.

---

## 13. Return or deletion of personal data

On termination or on Controller's instruction, Processor will, at Controller's
choice:

1. **Return** the personal data to Controller in a machine-readable format
   (`tar`/`parquet` plus signed manifest); or
2. **Delete** the personal data through the ordinary DSR/erasure pipeline.
   BYOK key revocation, crypto-erase, and a BYOK kill switch are unavailable
   at launch; no five-minute timing commitment or NIST-equivalence claim is made.

Processor will provide an **erasure attestation** signed with the per-region
attestation key (`INV-ERASURE-ATTESTATION-SIGNED`). Legal retention
obligations under §5 survive.

## 14. Liability

Liability is governed by the master agreement, subject to mandatory law.
Nothing in this DPA limits liability for: (i) personal injury or death caused
by negligence; (ii) gross negligence or willful misconduct; (iii) breach of
confidentiality; or (iv) any liability that cannot lawfully be limited.
