# PCI DSS

> **The short version:** **CoreLink is PCI DSS SAQ-A compliant. We never
> see your card data.** When you enter a card, it goes straight from your
> browser into Stripe's PCI-validated infrastructure; CoreLink only ever
> stores opaque Stripe tokens (`pm_…`, `cus_…`, `sub_…`).

## Posture at a glance

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Compliance level | **SAQ-A** (the simplest PCI-DSS self-assessment, for merchants who outsource 100% of cardholder-data handling) |
| PCI DSS version | **v4.0** |
| Latest self-assessment | **2026-05-15** (validity: 2026-05-15 → 2027-05-14) |
| Next recertification | **2027-05-15** (annual cadence) |
| Card data stored on CoreLink? | **No.** Zero. Never. |
| Card data transmitted by CoreLink? | **No.** All card capture is direct-to-Stripe via Stripe Elements. |
| Card data processed by CoreLink? | **No.** Stripe handles authorization, capture, settlement, recurring billing, disputes. |
| What CoreLink does store | Opaque Stripe identifiers only: `customer_id` (`cus_…`), `subscription_id` (`sub_…`), `payment_method_id` (`pm_…`), `invoice_id` (`in_…`), and the audit-log event IDs (`evt_…`). Plus last-4 + brand for display purposes (not CHD per PCI DSS §3.3.1.1). |
| Payment service provider | **Stripe, Inc.** — PCI DSS Level 1 Service Provider (SAQ-D-SP), on Visa Global Registry of Service Providers and Mastercard Compliant Service Provider List. |

## Why SAQ-A is the right scoping

PCI DSS v4.0 defines nine "SAQ" (Self-Assessment Questionnaire) variants
depending on how much of the cardholder-data environment (CDE) a merchant
operates itself. SAQ-A is the *simplest* — it applies when:

1. The merchant accepts only card-not-present transactions.
2. **All** cardholder-data functions are outsourced to a PCI-validated
   third-party service provider.
3. The merchant never electronically stores, processes, or transmits
   cardholder data.
4. The payment page is served by (or iframed from) the validated TPSP.

CoreLink meets all four. We deliberately engineered our billing surface so
that cardholder data never crosses our network boundary.

## What you can verify yourself

You don't have to take our word for it. Anyone can run the following
checks against the public repo to verify the zero-cardholder-data claim:

```bash
# 1. Grep the codebase for card-data field names outside test/spec docs.
grep -rEn 'card_number|cvv|cvc|\bpan\b' \
  --include='*.rs' --include='*.ts' --include='*.tsx' --include='*.py' \
  --include='*.go' --include='*.js' \
  | grep -vE '/tests/|/test/|/specs/|_test\.|\.test\.'

# Expected: hits only in crates/corelink-telemetry/src/logpush/redaction.rs
# (a defense-in-depth log-redactor that detects and scrubs accidentally
#  leaked card numbers before logs leave our infrastructure — see the
#  note in the SAQ-A doc).

# 2. Inspect the Content-Security-Policy every CoreLink console page carries,
#    on the rendered public pricing page where an upgrade to a paid tier
#    starts. Card entry itself happens on Stripe-hosted Checkout, not on our
#    pages. Fetch the page itself: the locale-less /corelink/pricing is only a
#    redirect, and a redirect's headers say nothing about the page behind it.
#    The first command refuses anything but a 200 HTML response, so the policy
#    the second prints is the one the rendered page was served with.
curl -sS -o /dev/null -D pricing-headers.txt -w '%{http_code} %{content_type}\n' \
  https://humangr.com/corelink/en/pricing | grep -E '^200 text/html' &&
  grep -i '^content-security-policy:' pricing-headers.txt

# Expected: `200 text/html; charset=utf-8`, then the policy
# (source: apps/admin-ui/src/lib/csp.ts): script-src allows 'self',
# a per-request nonce, and exactly four third-party hosts — Clerk
# (clerk.corelink-app.humangr.com), Cloudflare Turnstile
# (challenges.cloudflare.com), Plausible analytics (plausible.io) and Stripe
# (js.stripe.com). No tag manager and no chat widget is allowed.
```

## What about Stripe?

Stripe is the single sub-processor in our PCI scope. Their compliance posture:

- **PCI DSS Level 1 Service Provider** (the highest tier; > 6M transactions/yr).
- **SAQ-D for Service Providers** (the most rigorous self-assessment).
- Listed on Visa Global Registry of Service Providers.
- Listed on Mastercard Compliant Service Provider List.
- Stripe AOC tracked annually in our vendor risk register
  ([see Sub-processors](./subprocessors)).

If Stripe's AOC ever lapses, our boundary claim doesn't change — but our
upstream coverage does, and we would publish a notice on this page within
24 hours.

## Frequently asked

**Do you take card-present (in-person) payments?**
No. CoreLink is a SaaS billed via card-not-present transactions only.

**Do you do MOTO (mail-order / telephone-order)?**
No.

**Do you run a Stripe Connect platform?**
No — we are a direct merchant, not a Stripe Connect platform. (If we ever
add Connect, we will re-scope to the appropriate SAQ variant, likely
SAQ-A-EP, and re-publish this page.)

**Can I get a copy of your AOC / SAQ-A?**
Yes — email `trust@humangr.com` with your DPA on file; we route signed
PDFs back within 1 business day.

**You store `last4` and `brand`. Isn't that CHD?**
No. Per PCI DSS v4.0 §3.3.1.1, the first-6 and last-4 digits of a PAN may
be displayed in clear; storing **only** last-4 + brand (with no middle
digits) is not CHD storage. We store it so we can show "Visa ending in
4242" in your customer portal.

**What about the logpush redaction module — why does it know what a PAN is?**
That module is *defense-in-depth*. It exists to detect and redact card
numbers from operational logs in case a misconfigured upstream client
ever POSTs a PAN to a wrong endpoint. It pattern-matches PANs, redacts
them with a salted-hash placeholder, and counts the redactions so we know
if a leak happened upstream. It never stores the original value. Property
test (100k synthetic PANs, zero leaks):
`crates/corelink-telemetry/tests/pii_redaction_100k_synthetic.rs`.
