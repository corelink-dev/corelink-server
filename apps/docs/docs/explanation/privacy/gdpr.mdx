# Your GDPR rights at CoreLink

> **DRAFT — pending Legal + DPO review.** Technical claims below are accurate as of May 15, 2026 and pinned to commit `de84b9d`. The companion compliance docs (`GDPR-FULL-AUDIT-2026-05-15.md`, `GDPR-SCC-EXECUTION-2026-05-15.md`, `GDPR-DPIA-LIBRARY.md`) are the source of truth; this page is the customer-facing summary. The sister page [LGPD rights](/privacy/lgpd-full) covers the parallel Brazilian regime; both regimes share most of the underlying machinery.

CoreLink is a Brazilian company building infrastructure used by European
developers, ML teams, and EU enterprises. Because we offer services to
people in the EU (Art. 3(2)(a)) and we run a sub-processor leg through
Neon's Frankfurt cluster (Art. 3(1)), **GDPR applies to us**. We treat
that obligation as a design constraint, not a compliance overlay.

## 1. The eight rights, our service level, how to exercise

GDPR Chapter III gives you eight concrete rights plus the right to
withdraw consent. We have implemented every one as a self-service API
endpoint. No support ticket needed; no human gate. The only friction is
WebAuthn step-up multifactor on destructive actions (Erasure and
Rectification) so nobody can hijack your account to delete your data.

| GDPR Art. | Right | Our SLA | How to exercise |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Art. 15** | Right of access (confirmation + copy of your data) | 5 business days (confirmation) / 15 business days (full export) — both inside the 1-month statutory cap | `POST /v1/privacy/dsr/access` |
| **Art. 16** | Right to rectification | 5 business days | `POST /v1/privacy/dsr/rectification` (MFA required) |
| **Art. 17** | Right to erasure ("right to be forgotten") | 30 calendar days | `POST /v1/privacy/dsr/erasure` (MFA required) |
| **Art. 18** | Right to restriction of processing | 5 business days | `POST /v1/privacy/dsr/restriction` |
| **Art. 19** | Notification of rectification/erasure/restriction to recipients | Automatic — same SLA as the triggering right | Triggered by Art. 16, 17, and 18 endpoints; recipient manifest in your audit |
| **Art. 20** | Right to data portability | 15 business days | `POST /v1/privacy/dsr/portability` |
| **Art. 21** | Right to object | 15 business days (Art. 21(1)) / **immediate** for direct marketing (Art. 21(2)) | `POST /v1/privacy/dsr/objection` |
| **Art. 22** | Right not to be subject to automated decisions with legal/significant effect | N/A — **we do not run Art. 22 processing** (see §7) | — |
| **Art. 7(3)** | Right to withdraw consent (as easy as giving) | 5 business days | email `privacy@humangr.com` — the self-service endpoint is not available yet |

If you prefer email, write to `privacy@hugr.com`. Our DPO will process
your request through the same pipeline with the same SLA. The clock
starts when we verify your identity, not when you sent the email — a
nuance that EDPB Guidelines 01/2022 §63 explicitly permits.

## 2. What data we hold

A short, honest answer.

**About you as a CoreLink user:** your email address, your name, your
billing address, your hashed password or WebAuthn public key, the IP
address and user-agent class of your recent requests (truncated and
pseudonymised), your DSR requests, your consent grants and revocations,
and your support tickets.

**About data your tenant uploads (blobs):** we hold the bytes you upload,
a content-addressable hash of those bytes (the digest), some metadata
(size, timestamp, your tenant ID as a random UUID), and an audit record
that says "this tenant uploaded this digest at this time" — **but we do
not look at the content of your blobs**. When your blob contains personal
data of someone else (for example, your customers' info embedded in a dataset),
**you are the controller and we are the processor** under our DPA
(Art. 28). We do not read or repurpose your data.

**What we don't hold:** payment card data (Stripe processes that, we
never see it); biometric templates (WebAuthn keys are public cryptographic
identifiers, not biometrics — the actual biometric never leaves your
device); data about children (CoreLink is a B2B platform; our Terms of
Service require account holders to be adults representing a business
entity).

The complete inventory of 14 dataflows is catalogued in our
[Record of Processing Activities](/privacy/sub-processors) (Art. 30).
Each one specifies the data category, purpose, legal basis, retention,
recipients, and security measures.

## 3. Why we are allowed to hold each piece (legal bases)

GDPR Art. 6(1) lists six lawful bases. We use four — **fixed per purpose**
so we cannot quietly swap your data from "consent" to "legitimate
interest" mid-flight. The immutability is enforced in code
(`crates/corelink-privacy-consent-ledger/`) and verified by 10,000
property-test cases per CI run plus a TLA+ specification of the
grant/revoke ledger symmetry (`InvConsentSymmetry`).

| Why we hold the data | GDPR base | Example |
|---|---|---|
| To run the platform you signed up for | Art. 6(1)(b) — contract | CAS uploads, AC entries, your account |
| Because the law requires it | Art. 6(1)(c) — legal obligation | Audit logs (SOC 2 + Art. 30), fiscal records (member-state-specific 6–10y), DSR fulfillment |
| To prevent abuse and fraud | Art. 6(1)(f) — legitimate interest | Security monitoring, anomaly detection |
| Because you said yes | Art. 6(1)(a) — consent | Marketing emails, personalised analytics, opt-in ML training, beta features |

If your data is held under consent, you can withdraw at any time and we
stop processing within 5 minutes. **We do not silently fall back to
"legitimate interest"** when your consent lapses — processing pauses
until you re-consent or we delete the data. This is intentional and
verifiable.

## 4. How fast we respond

The clock starts when we verify your identity (WebAuthn step-up for
Erasure and Rectification; session re-auth for Access and Portability).
Art. 12(3) gives us up to one month, extendable by two further months
for complex requests — we have committed to faster SLAs than the
statutory cap for every right.

We can pause the clock in three narrow cases:

- You are under a **legal hold** (court order, regulatory subpoena,
  ongoing investigation). We tell you the request is paused and why.
- Your request is **ambiguous** and we asked for clarification.
- **Technical impossibility** documented in our incident records. Cap of
  5 business days per request before customer notification.

Every state transition (`verified`, `in_progress`, `completed`,
`rejected`) sends you a confirmation email. Receipts are RS256 JWTs
signed with our KMS-rooted key (90-day anti-replay) so you can prove
later that the request happened.

If we reject a request as "manifestly unfounded or excessive" (Art. 12(5)
— a high bar we rarely use), we explain why and you have the right to
lodge a complaint with your local supervisory authority and/or seek a
judicial remedy.

## 5. The deletion ("erasure") in detail

When you exercise Art. 17, the erasure orchestrator evaluates a canonical
list of 12 backend kinds. Only **4 have a real, shipped adapter** — those
are where your data actually lives — and each is genuinely erased or
pseudonymised:

- **D1** — subject-scoped rows deleted; refcount synced with R2.
- **R2 CAS** (content-addressable storage) — refcount-aware eraser: if
  your blob is shared with other subjects, we drop your reference and
  keep the blob until every reference is gone (plus a 72-hour grace
  window); if it's referenced only by you, we tombstone it immediately.
- **R2 AC** (Action Cache) — entries owned by your tenant are deleted.
- **Stripe** — your `Customer` record is updated with email, name, and
  address nullified. Stripe does not support full deletion because it
  would break invoice integrity, so this backend is **pseudonymised
  retention**, not erasure.

The remaining 8 canonical backend kinds are reconciled to
`not_applicable` rather than silently reported as an erased no-op,
because no durable subject data exists there in the shipped system
(ADR-S11-013, cold-verified 2026-06-11):

| Back end | Why it's not applicable |
|---|---|
| Neon control plane | Neon control-plane not shipped; subject identity lives in D1 (handled by the D1 adapter) |
| Neon billing detail | Neon billing not shipped; billing state lives in D1 + Stripe |
| Neon PITR backup | Neon PITR not shipped; no Neon backups to tombstone |
| KV | KV namespaces are caches (metadata/JWKS/negative-cache); no durable PII, reconstructed from D1 |
| Loki / Grafana | No active Loki sink shipped (observability references only) |
| R2 audit (subject to pseudonymisation) | No R2 audit WORM bucket shipped; no subject-indexed audit store to apply pseudonymisation to |
| R2 CAS legal-hold partition | No legal-hold CAS partition shipped |
| R2 evidence store | No R2 evidence-* bucket shipped |

If any of these ever ships with real subject data, its row switches from
`not_applicable` to a real erase/pseudonymise outcome — the per-backend
audit record makes that transition visible rather than silent.

You receive a `dsr.completed.v1` event carrying a signed evidence hash
that binds all 12 per-backend outcomes (four real, eight
`not_applicable`), plus a confirmation email. Total SLA: 30 calendar
days.

## 6. Where your data goes (international transfers)

Most of your data stays in the EU. CoreLink defaults to residency-pinned
EU edges for EU-tenant blob workloads, EU audit chain, EU telemetry, and
EU-region BYOK. The exceptions cross the Atlantic and are governed by
either the **EU-US Data Privacy Framework (DPF)** adequacy decision (10
July 2023) or the **EU Standard Contractual Clauses 2021/914 Module 2**
with a Schrems II Transfer Impact Assessment (TIA) on file.

| Recipient | Country | Mechanism | What's transferred |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cloudflare | EU edges by default | Intra-EU (adequacy) | Workers / R2 / D1 / KV / DO for EU subjects |
| Neon | EU (DE) for EU subjects | Intra-EU (adequacy) | Account, billing, consent, DSR ticket rows |
| Stripe | US | **EU-US DPF (primary)** + SCC Module 2 (fallback) + Art. 49(1)(b) (belt-and-suspenders) | Email, pseudonymised customer ID, amount — **no card data ever touches CoreLink** |
| Clerk | US | EU-US DPF (primary) + SCC Module 2 (fallback) | Auth tokens + email only |
| AWS / GCP / Azure (BYOK) | EU regions for EU tenants | Intra-EU (adequacy) | Envelope key URI only; HuGR never sees key material |
| Grafana Labs | EU (Frankfurt) | Intra-EU (adequacy) | Pseudonymised metrics for EU tenants |
| PagerDuty / GitHub | US | DPF + SCC + sub-processor-specific posture | Operational metadata; no end-user PII |

Sentry, PostHog, and LogRocket are **disabled by default for EU
subjects** pending a sub-processor review (tracked as GAP-14). To enable
them for your data, you would opt in with explicit consent and we would
publish the corresponding TIA.

Our full SCC execution status, Schrems II TIA register, and the
contingency plan for a hypothetical "Schrems-III" challenge to the DPF
are documented in the [Transfer Impact Assessment register](/privacy/sub-processors).
We pre-staged SCC Module 2 in every US sub-processor DPA so a DPF
challenge would not require renegotiation — only an activation switch.

## 7. Automated decisions and profiling (Art. 22)

We do not run any automated decision-making with legal or similarly
significant effect on you. The closest activity is the
`training_ml_models` opt-in purpose (a backend optimisation that predicts
cache hit probability for build artifacts) which:

- Is opt-in only with default `false`.
- Uses a k≥50 anonymity floor.
- Has no decision arm that affects you (the prediction is invisible to
  you and changes only internal cache pre-warming).

If we ever ship a feature that crosses the Art. 22 threshold, our DPIA
trigger catalogue forces a Data Protection Impact Assessment before
launch, and you would receive the Art. 13(2)(f) disclosure in a new
version of our privacy notice (which would re-prompt you for consent
under the rules of our [consent ledger](/privacy/lgpd-full#7-what-about-consent)).

## 8. Special situations

**You are an EU subject whose data was uploaded by a tenant.** You can
file a DSR with us. We route the request to the tenant who controls that
data under our DPA (Art. 28) and notify you within 5 business days.
The tenant has a 30-day SLA. If they do not respond, you can escalate to
your supervisory authority with our routing record.

**Joint controllership (Art. 26).** We currently do not operate any
Art. 26 joint controllership arrangement. If a future co-branded EU
launch creates one, we publish the arrangement transparently and offer
each subject a single point of contact (per Art. 26(2)).

**Children's data (Art. 8).** Out of scope. CoreLink is B2B; our Terms
of Service require account holders to be ≥ 18 representing a business.
If a tenant uploads data about minors, the tenant is the controller and
the Art. 8 obligations sit with them, not with us.

**Data triggering another subject's privacy.** We redact references to
other subjects in your export bundle. This is mandatory under Art. 5(1)(c)
data minimisation in disclosure.

## 9. Incidents and breach notification

GDPR Art. 33 gives us **72 hours** to notify the supervisory authority
from awareness of a personal data breach. Art. 34 obliges us to notify
you "without undue delay" when the breach is likely to result in high
risk to your rights and freedoms. Our internal timeline is conservative
versus both:

- Detection → internal SEV-1 declaration: ≤ 1 hour.
- Declaration → triage and containment: ≤ 4 hours.
- Awareness → supervisory authority notification: **≤ 48 hours** (beating
  the 72h statutory cap).
- SA notification → notification to affected subjects: ≤ 72 hours.
- Public post-mortem (if material): ≤ 14 days.

We tabletop-exercise this twice a year. Notifications reach you by email
plus an in-app banner if you have an active session, with a structured
disclosure of breach type, categories of data, likely consequences, and
mitigations under way (Art. 33(3) content list).

## 10. Who is responsible (DPO and EU representative)

Our Data Protection Officer (Art. 37) handles all of this. Formal DPO
appointment lands before EU enterprise customer onboarding; until then,
the interim Privacy Officer is Gustavo Schneiter (`gustavo@humangr.com`
direct, `privacy@hugr.com` for the DPO inbox).

We appoint an EU representative under Art. 27 before our first
EU-established Lighthouse customer goes live. The representative
candidate firms are documented in `legal/legal-externo-engagement-contract.md`
and the appointment is tracked in our public roadmap.

Your supervisory authority is the data-protection regulator in your EU
member state of residence. If you believe we are not honouring your
GDPR rights, you have the right to lodge a complaint with them
(Art. 77) and the right to a judicial remedy (Art. 79). Once our EU
representative is appointed, we operate under the one-stop-shop
mechanism (Art. 56) with a single lead supervisory authority for
cross-border complaints.
